# Spot check: Bluster Rock (Who's trying to convince me?)

These are the 10 Encounters the automatic checks were least sure about. For each, tick **OK**, or write what is wrong. Two right answers, a confusing prompt, or a wrong answer are all worth a note.

## 1. 3.3-b4-2 (Band 4)

> BRING BACK THE FESTIVAL: an opinion piece by Jojo Reyes of Red Dust Static. The Bluster Rock Music Festival has not been held since 2019, and I believe the council should bring it back next autumn. Some people say a festival is too noisy, too expensive and too much trouble for a small town. I understand those worries, and I don't want to pretend they aren't real. Like many people my age, I first heard live music at that festival, sitting on my dad's shoulders in the dust and watching a bush band play until midnight. Our band still has a faded festival T-shirt from that year, and our drummer wears it to every gig for luck. But I have spent the last month gathering backing for my claim that a festival would be good for Bluster Rock, and I think the case is strong. I spoke to shopkeepers, read the council's own reports, interviewed people who study tourism and music, and listened to locals who remember the old festivals. Below are six pieces of the backing I found. I hope that readers will judge each one on its merits. If the council agrees, three local bands have already offered to play for free, and the old flatbed truck behind the footy club could serve as a stage.

**Match each piece of backing to its kind.**

- The council's own report shows that 6,000 visitors came to the 2019 festival. → **evidence**
- Professor Lena Watts, who researches tourism in country towns, says festivals are one of the best ways to bring visitors back. → **expert**
- After playing at the 2019 festival, a local singer, Tash Moore, was offered her first recording contract. → **example**
- A survey of 300 residents found that two-thirds would like the festival to return. → **evidence**
- Mr Victor Lam, who has organised outback festivals for fifteen years, says noise can be managed with careful planning. → **expert**
- The bakery's owner, Rosa, remembers selling out of pies by ten o'clock on the festival's first morning. → **example**

Why: Takings, reports and surveys are evidence. Professors and planners are experts. Single stories, however moving, are examples.

## 4. 3.2-b2-1 (Band 2)

> This week, the Bugle asked readers about the Bluster Rock Primary school band. Should it get more money from the council? Here are some of the claims readers sent in. Some readers gave reasons. Others just said what they thought, and then said it again. One reader sent in a drawing of a trombone, which we have not printed. Last year, the band played at the Bluster Rock Show, the town's birthday picnic and the opening of the new skate park. The council will decide next month. Until then, the band will keep practising in the old shed behind the oval, where the roof leaks every time it rains.

**Put each reader's claim in the right box.**

- The band deserves more money. It won the state band competition this year. → **backed-up claim: a reason or proof that supports it**
- The band needs new instruments. Half the trumpets have holes in them. → **backed-up claim: a reason or proof that supports it**
- The band is fantastic. It has 24 players in red jackets. → **bare claim: nothing supports it**
- The council must give the band more money. Do it now! → **bare claim: nothing supports it**
- The band brings people together. Over 500 people came to its last concert. → **backed-up claim: a reason or proof that supports it**

Why: Backed-up claims give proof that fits the claim. Bare claims repeat themselves, give orders, or add facts that don't fit.

## 5. 3.4-b2-6 (Band 2)

> REVIEW: SLICK VINNIE'S GHOST TOUR. Two stars. My family went on Slick Vinnie's ghost tour last Saturday. We met at the mine gate at 8 pm. The night was cold and windy, and we did not bring jumpers. The tour cost $5 each, and there were twelve people in our group. Vinnie carried a lantern and wore a long black coat. The miserable tour dragged on for three hours in the cold. Vinnie told the same ghost story four times. My son saw a glowing light in a tunnel, but it was Vinnie's phone. We did get a free hot chocolate at the end. From a tourist from Saltbush Creek

**Build a neutral version of the sentence about how long the tour went.**

Pieces: The | miserable | tour | dragged on | went | for three hours | in the cold | .

Answer: **The tour went for three hours in the cold.** or **The tour went in the cold for three hours.**

Why: 'Went for three hours' keeps the facts. 'Miserable' and 'dragged on' were there to make you feel bored.

> ALBUM REVIEW: 'RED DIRT ROAD' by The Dusty Possums. Posted this week. Let me be clear: this is the greatest album ever recorded on Bluster Rock, and possibly anywhere. From the first twang of the banjo to the final crash of the drums, every one of its twelve songs is a number-one hit waiting to happen. The band formed four years ago in a shearing shed outside town, and this is their second album. The drumming on 'Galah Galah' is so fast that I worried the drummer might take off. **Tap the part Zed got wrong, then choose a better one.**

Text: "The review is recent, because it was posted this week. But it's hard to trust, because the writer is a music critic who earns money when albums sell."

Why: Jett Ramone is the band's manager. He's paid more when more albums sell, so he gains from a rave review.
