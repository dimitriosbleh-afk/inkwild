# First Nations stories and texts in a public literacy game: what a Victorian government school can use

Research date: 4 November 2026. Not legal advice. **[Inference]** marks the researcher's reading of a source; everything else is attributed.

## Bottom line

- **No published First Nations story can go into the game as-is without consultation.** Two separate tests must both pass: **copyright** (a licence to publish the text) and **cultural protocol / ICIP** (Traditional Owner agreement). ICIP rights persist even where copyright has lapsed.
- **Best copyright-clean candidate: the Living Archive of Aboriginal Languages** (CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 AU): unaltered, attributed, non-commercial reproduction is allowed. But it asks users to follow ICIP/ATSILIRN protocols, forbids modification, and its stories are mostly from outside Victoria, so protocols still point to consultation first.
- **Link or use outside the game only:** ABC Education *Dust Echoes* (ABC terms bar republishing; ABC tags it Years 7-10), AIATSIS web content (linking allowed, redistribution and framing not), Creative Victoria/VACL *Nyernila* (Copyright Act only), Indigenous Literacy Foundation (all rights reserved), Story Box Library (subscription), Magabala titles. **[Inference]** Links do not work in an offline game; the practical pattern is the class reads/views with the teacher and the game holds only the teacher's own response tasks.
- **Putting any story text, audio or adaptation inside a public game needs permission.** The Department's Koorie Cross-Curricular Protocols require consultation with the Traditional Owners of the school's land (Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung for Diamond Creek) for any activity that produces or replicates a cultural expression (stories explicitly included). Publishers also need copyright permission (Magabala: fee, up to 4 weeks).
- **The school's statutory copyright licence does not cover a public GitHub Pages app.** Smartcopying and Department guidance confine statutory-licence copies to password-protected / internal use.
- **Start with:** the regional Koorie Education Coordinator (KEC) / Koorie Engagement Support Officer (KESO), the Local Aboriginal Education Consultative Group (LAECG, via VAEAI), and the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Cultural Heritage Aboriginal Corporation education unit (Education@wurundjeri.com.au).

## 1. Victorian protocols

- **Department of Education, Koorie Cross-Curricular Protocols** (VCAA copy, Dec 2017): https://victoriancurriculum.vcaa.vic.edu.au/static/docs/Koorie%20Cross-Curricular%20Protocols.pdf (live page timed out: https://www.education.vic.gov.au/school/teachers/teachingresources/multicultural/Pages/koorieculture.aspx). Cultural expressions (stories, poetry, songs) are Koorie intellectual property. Producing or replicating one requires consultation with the Traditional Owners of the school's land; receptive activities (reading/viewing credible public resources) do not. Local content first. **[Inference]** Re-presenting a story inside a game the teacher publishes is closer to replication, so consultation applies.
- **VCAA**, *Learning about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures*: https://victoriancurriculum.vcaa.vic.edu.au/static/docs/Learning%20about%20Aboriginal%20and%20Torres%20Strait%20Islander%20histories%20and%20cultures.pdf (v1.0-era; points to the Department and VAEAI protocols). VC v2.0 English names First Nations literature at every level (VC2E5LE01, VC2E6LE01).
- **VAEAI, Protocols for Koorie Education in Victorian Primary and Secondary Schools**: https://aev.vic.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Protocols-for-Koorie-Education-in-Victorian-Primary-and-Secondary-Schools-2019.pdf. Local Koorie content should be endorsed by the LAECG; resources must be developed by or with Koorie representatives, with permission, attribution and preferably a written agreement. Respect gender-restricted material.
- **Department Koorie Education policy** (Oct 2026): https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/koorie-education/policy. KECs/KESOs are the contacts for cultural inclusion.
- **Creative Australia, Protocols for using First Nations Cultural and Intellectual Property in the Arts** (3rd ed., 2019): https://creative.gov.au/investment-and-development/protocols-and-resources/protocols-for-using-first-nations-cultural-and-intellectual-property-in-the-arts/. Ten principles incl. free, prior, informed consent; attribution; benefit sharing. Permission may be needed even for public-domain stories; adapting needs both copyright and cultural permission; consent before digitising/putting online.
- **Smartcopying on ICIP**: https://smartcopying.edu.au/guidelines/copyright-basics/indigenous-cultural-and-intellectual-property-rights/ (checklist asks for written consent from traditional owners/custodians).
- **Aboriginal Heritage Act 2006 (Vic) Part 5A**: https://www.firstpeoplesrelations.vic.gov.au/protecting-aboriginal-intangible-heritage (registered intangible heritage, commercial use). Not checked whether any Wurundjeri stories are registered.

## 3. Statutory licence and a public web app

Does not cover it. Smartcopying Text FAQ (Q11: public website needs owner permission): https://smartcopying.edu.au/faqs/text-works/ ; resource-developer guide (public sites only for owned, CC, public domain or permitted material; linking is fine): https://smartcopying.edu.au/creating-learning-and-teaching-resources-a-copyright-guide-for-tafe-resource-developers/ ; Department PAL: https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/intellectual-property-and-copyright/guidance/using-copyright-material and https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/intellectual-property-and-copyright/policy (staff-created material is Department copyright, usually released under Creative Commons).
