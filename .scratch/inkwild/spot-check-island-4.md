## 1. 4.1-b4-2 (Band 4)

> Experiment 14: the Mangrove Mud Monster. I mixed three cups of riverbank mud with glue, a sprinkle of borax and a spoonful of Mo's leftover gravy, which nobody else was going to eat. Then I strained out all the mud through one of Zed's socks. Within an hour, the mixture had become a single blob, so clear that I could read the ruler lying underneath it. When I poked it with a pencil, it stretched into long, gooey strings that took ages to snap. When I tilted the tray, the blob began to slide towards the edge. It moved so slowly that it took nearly ten minutes to reach the rim. Mo said it was the best gravy I had ever made. Now I need one simile for my report, so I have written four drafts. I want the one that fits everything I noticed about Kevin, which is what I have named the blob. Zed says I should pick the funniest one. I have told Zed that this is science, and that I still have his sock.

**Tap the draft that fits everything Gus noticed about the blob.**

Tap: **It oozed like cold honey off a spoon.** in "Draft 1: It crept along like a sleepy turtle. Draft 2: It oozed like cold honey off a spoon. Draft 3: It zipped like a raindrop down a window. Draft 4: It poured like wet cement from a mixer."

Why: Cold honey is slow, see-through and stringy. It's the only draft that matches all three things Gus noticed.

> By midnight, the storm had turned the bay into something none of the crew had seen before. **Tully crossed out a cliché. Which fresh picture would do the most for someone reading the ship's log?**

Why: Measuring each wave against their own mast lets the reader see it towering over the boat and crew.

## 5. 4.7-b3-3 (Band 3)

> REEF DIVE
by Poet Pip

We roll backwards off the boat,
into a world of green and blue.
The chain clanks against the hull.
Then everything goes quiet.
Pale coral, chalky white and cinnamon brown,
stretches out beneath us.

Bright blue butterflyfish bob below.
Kelp curls and coils in the current.
We float above the coral bed.
A turtle bumps me on the head.

An octopus peers out of a crack,
then squeezes in and won't come back.
Our air runs low, it's time to rise.
We climb aboard with salty eyes.

Poet Pip says she wrote the poem on the boat ride home, with a wet towel round her shoulders, and she read it out loud to herself again and again. 'A poem isn't finished,' she says, 'until it sounds right in your mouth.' She changed one line six times before she was happy with it.

**Read each line aloud and match it to its sound device. Listen, don't just look.**

- Bright blue butterflyfish bob below. → **alliteration**
- Pale coral, chalky white and cinnamon brown. → **no sound device**
- The chain clanks against the hull. → **onomatopoeia**
- Kelp curls and coils in the current. → **alliteration**
- We float above the coral bed. A turtle bumps me on the head. → **rhyme**
- Our air runs low, it's time to rise. We climb aboard with salty eyes. → **rhyme**

Why: Kelp and coils share a k sound. Coral, chalky and cinnamon look alike but start with three different sounds.
