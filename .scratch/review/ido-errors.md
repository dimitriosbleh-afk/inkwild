# I do lines that teach something wrong or leave a gap

The Wave 1 fixers left these alone because the I do is rewritten in Wave 2. Every rewritten I do must fix the items listed for its Skill.

## Island 1 and 2
- **2.8 beats 2–3:** they call the first trouble "the problem" and the complication "what makes it even worse". The complication is the problem: orientation, complication, resolution.
- **2.5 beat 3:** "the want plus the problem is the dilemma" is wrong. A dilemma is a choice between two wants.
- **2.2:** "only knows what they see, hear or get told" is true only of an "I" narrator. A third-person narrator can know thoughts (2.1-b3-1).
- **1.6 beat 1:** it defines the topic, not the main idea. Show a funny detail that still backs up the main idea.
- **2.9 beat 3:** it ties long sentences to colourful detail.
- **Missing teaching:**
  - 1.9 never shows how two texts relate: an example, an update or a correction.
  - 1.5 never shows contrast clues.
  - 1.8 never defines "contradiction".
  - 1.7 beat 3's "fits any story" no longer matches the new too-broad options.
  - 2.1 never shows "we", "our" or "us".
- **Island 2:** introduce "narrator" ("the narrator, the one telling the story") and use it from then on.

## Island 3 and 4
- **3.1 beat 5:** "False, but you can check it. Fact." It should be a "fact claim".
- **3.3:** it says evidence is numbers or records. Experts and examples are evidence too. Teach strong evidence against weak evidence, and checkable against not checkable.
- **3.4 beat 5:** "Fixed is wrong!" has no example.
- **3.9:** "must" is taught only as "no choice", but 3.9-b4-6 uses it to mean certain. Name high, medium and low modality.
- **4.10 beats 4–5:** the frozen meat pie comes from outside the scene. Use something in the scene, for example "the metal seats in the stand".
- **4.4 how:** "can't be literally true" is wrong.
- **4.5:** never says hyperbole isn't a lie. Link it to 3.7's exaggeration.
- **4.2:** teaches only the "X is Y" metaphor.
- **3.7, 3.10:** name the counter-argument and the rebuttal.

## Island 5
- **5.1:** never teaches a phrase against a clause.
- **5.2:** "Say this part alone. Does it work?" fails on questions.
- **5.3 beat 4:** "No footy unless the rain stops" has no verb in its main part, and "until" and "unless" are crammed into one beat.
- **5.4 why:** teaches only one effect.
- **5.5 beat 2:** the lift-it-out test belongs to clauses with commas, but the example has no commas.
- **5.6 beat 5:** "the noun stays at the end" stops being true once 5.7 adds detail after the noun.
- **5.7 beat 3:** says "no action word" where the island says "verb".
- **5.8 beat 4:** "steaming" changes the meaning rather than sharpening it.
- **5.9:** "evaporates when you heat it" is misleading, and its why line says "to sound like an expert".
- **5.12:** never teaches had, has/have, was + -ing, or the plain present for habits.
- **5.13 beat 1:** "one whole thought" is contradicted by beat 4. 5.13 should also unlock first for weak readers.
- **Never taught:** "whose" (5.7-b4-5) and "provided that" (5.3 Band 4).
- **Bridge once:**
  - "doer" (subject)
  - "joining word" (conjunction)
  - "main clause" (independent clause)
- **5.11:** "adverb group" or "adverbial" is an open question for James.

## Island 6 and 7
- **6.5 beat 2:** "Lift it out. The sentence still works." fails on clauses that tell you which one.
- **6.10 beat 4:** "Spoken words start with a capital letter" is untrue for split speech.
- **6.9 beat 3:** link "its" to "his" and "her".
- **6.7 beat 4:** show the "Three possum's" trap.
- **6.1 beat 1:** "one whole thought". Show a comma splice.
- **7.3 beat 2:** each step is built around a command verb, often first but not always.
- **7.5 beat 1 and why:** "Readers notice the start most" has no evidence behind it. Beat 3 calls a "Because…" opener hiding the message, which clashes with 6.4.

## Island 8 and 9
- **8.1:** "The doer should come first" is wrong, because passive sentences are correct. Its missing-word example highlights "the".
- **8.2:** "A sentence is one whole thought". Show the comma splice and "however".
- **8.3:** "A story picks a time and sticks to it". Some verbs rightly stay present.
- **8.4:** "Repeats make readers drift off" clashes with 7.10's repetition that holds a text together.
- **8.7:** teaches only end marks and capitals, but its tasks test its/it's, extra-detail commas and split speech.
- **9.1:** a beat teaches the "Have you ever…?" opener.
- **9.10:** an exposé or an exposition?
- **9.5:** "raises the stakes" and "up the ladder" are idioms that EAL learners will misread.
