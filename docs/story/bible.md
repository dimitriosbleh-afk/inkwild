# MAXX OUT: story bible

The year-long story in Chapters. Read this before writing any Chapter. Content rules (Australian spelling, no real brands or people, no First Nations stories) are in `docs/agents/content-writing.md`; length and sentence targets per Band are `CHAPTER_WORDS` and `BAND_TARGETS` in `src/content/rules.ts`.

**Premise.** Maxx Vantablack, the world's biggest streamer, is secretly capturing every rare Creature in the archipelago to star in his channel. His 40 million followers, the Vantafam, think he is rescuing them. The crew sails the leaky *Soggy Biscuit* from Island to Island, pulls a Heist on each of Maxx's bases, frees the Creatures and collects evidence that he is lying. In the finale they hijack his live stream to prove it.

**Keepers (all eight written):** **Captain Clause** (he), a huge old sea captain with a belt-length white beard: "I am not THAT Claus. Mine has an E on the end." He refuses to answer fragments ("That is not a sentence. That is a crumb. Who did what?"). Clawsette, a small blue crab, rides on his shoulder snipping. **Inspector Fennick** (she), long coat, frying-pan-sized magnifying glass, reads everyone at a glance, and never says anything directly: "I couldn't possibly say... but notice..." Owlibi, her owl, was always somewhere else the whole time. **Madame Pause** (she), tall, silver-haired ski instructor with a scarf so long it trails like a tail; she says her punctuation out loud ("Welcome, comma, Captain, full stop.") and is horrified by "Let's eat Madame Pause!" Stoppit, a round robin with a red chest like a stop sign, rides on her ski pole and pecks anyone whose sentence won't end. **Narra the Teller** (he), tall and barefoot, in a cloak stitched from old sails, each patch printed with a story; he talks about himself in the third person like a narrator ("Narra the Teller does not trust them. Not yet."), asks "Whose story is it?" and retells any event from the other side. Echobat, a tiny bat hanging from his straw hat, repeats the last word of everything ("Story," squeaks Echobat. "Story.") and can copy any voice it has heard often, including Maxx's. **Slick Vinnie** (he), market spruiker at the bottom of Bluster Rock: red-and-white striped suit, slicked-back hair, moustache waxed into two curls, a big red umbrella for a stall. He also runs a ghost tour of the old mine at the back of the rock. Everything he says is a sales pitch built from persuasive tricks ("Roll up, roll up!", "Ten thousand sold this morning!", "Everybody's wearing one!", "Can you really afford NOT to?"), and he ends with "Would I lie to you?" He respects only customers who don't buy. Spruikoo, his kookaburra, cackles "Koo-koo-ha-ha-HA!" whenever someone makes a claim they can't back up, which is terrible for business. **Poet Pip** (she), small, round misted-up glasses, a yellow raincoat covered in handwritten poems, a pencil behind each ear; paddles a tin bathtub with a broom. She never says anything plainly: every line is a simile or metaphor ("Your boat is a soggy sandwich, and you are the filling."), followed by "Picture it." When she does speak plainly, it means something is badly wrong. Similoon, a fluffy grey long-necked bird that nests on her head, turns into whatever she is compared to ("as round as a ball", "as fierce as a crocodile"), so the crew must be careful with similes near her. **The Architect** (she), the builder of every scaffold on Scaffold Peaks: orange hi-vis vest over a puffer jacket, tape measure on her belt, a rolled-up blueprint under one arm; she abseils rather than climbs. She structures everything she says out loud ("I will say three things. First, good morning. Second, this is my island. Third, who are you? In conclusion, explain yourselves.") and stops anyone whose talk "does not hang together": "Start with your main point. Then your reasons." Her verdicts end "In conclusion, ..." ("In conclusion, not yet."). When Girderoo is taken, her words come out in no order at all. Girderoo, a rock-wallaby in a tiny yellow hard hat, stacks things from biggest to smallest and thumps his foot when something is in the wrong order. **Chief Editor Krak** (he) runs the *Redpen Rumble* newspaper ("ALL THE NEWS THAT'S TRUE, AND NOTHING THAT ISN'T") from a crooked wooden office on the crater rim: enormous, eyebrows like burnt toast, green visor, sleeves stained red to the elbow, a red pen as big as a carrot behind his ear. He edits his own speech and everyone else's as he goes ("you lovely... strike 'lovely'... you suspicious strangers"; "Cut two of those 'really's.") and says "Read it back!" His grudge: he read out his headline "MAXX VANTABLACK IS NOT A HERO", and the Edit Bay cut the NOT, so a video of him saying "Maxx Vantablack is... a hero!" has twenty million views. Scorchquill, a small black cockatoo whose crest feathers glow red-hot at the tips, burns a neat line through any mistake and can't fly past one. Each Keeper needs one verbal habit tied to their Branch's Skill.

## Islands, bases and evidence

BOLT-9 and Kaydence don't appear in person, and the twist Chapters say why: in "The Family Special" Kaydence says she'll film from the *Clickbait* ("Heists are terrible for my hair"), and in "Very Good, Sir. No." BOLT-9 says he'll guard the boat on heists and rate the leak. Wren *seeds* are fine (see below). The Keeper starts as a wary rival and ends as an ally once you've won the badge.

You spot a hatch in the back wall with four bolts stamped THIRD, IN CONCLUSION, FIRST, SECOND: she designed the office before Maxx locked her out ("Every building I draw has a second way in"), so it opens the way she talks. The Goldies the keypad called arrive just in time for the ladder-slide escape.
- **Bluster Rock: told by a Creature.** A Bilbolster kept as LIVE MERCH narrates in the first person. It calls you "the Captain", doesn't know the crew's names at first (Vinnie is "the man in the striped suit"), and its ears flop at every claim with nothing holding it up and stand up at proof. It stows away in the Captain's bag to hear the ledger's last page.
- **Comma Cay: starts in the middle.** It opens on "Stop them bots!", then goes back twenty minutes, catches up, and you get the winning line: "He forgot the comma. Watch."
- **Redpen Volcano: the Keeper surprises the crew.** Krak says only "I will meet you inside." He has been working in the Edit Bay for a week as its worst-ever night editor, sits up from among the snoring editors, and opens the drawer himself ("Password's FLAWLESSME"). Tully's footy score at the worst moment is left out of the Scaffold Peaks, Bluster Rock and Comma Cay Heists, so it still surprises where it does come.

Inkwell Bay has no Keeper and no badge, so its Chapters skip the wary-rival-to-ally arc; its arrive Chapter unlocks on first playing the Island and its Heist when the route is finished. ## Main story

- **Prologue** (`start`), "Maxx It Out!": the crew spots Maxx's own logo on a "rescue" net. Wren has a map of eight bases she "found". You decide to break them all out. Hook: Maxx holds up a photo of the *Soggy Biscuit*: "See you soon, Captain."
- **Old Glimmer.** A whale-sized glowing ray nobody has seen for fifty years. Only one Heist reveals her as news: the Wishlist (Quillstone) names her as "THE BIG ONE" for Maxx's live finale, and Captain Clause says "Old Glimmer is real... Nobody has seen her in fifty years. And Maxx is hunting her." "Fifty years" and "is real" belong to that Chapter alone. Every other Heist builds on her with one new fact, and still works if read first by naming her in passing with a short gloss ("Old Glimmer, the great glowing ray from the old sea stories"), never with a gasp of discovery:
  - Echo Studio (Twotide): the last tape is her deep, slow song, labelled "THE BIG ONE. Play this to lure her close. Bring the biggest net." Narra listens and says, "She is singing to someone. Someone small." (seeds the baby).
  - Set photos (Mirage Mangroves): the last photo, taken from the *Vantadome*'s deck, shows Maxx already knows where she is. Pip, for once, says it plainly: "He knows where she is."
  - Blueprints (Scaffold Peaks): a lake-sized tank, "Catch her live on opening night!" The Architect: "In conclusion, we have until opening night."
  - Ledger (Bluster Rock) and last clip (Redpen Volcano) only hint: the biggest net ever made, and a finale that is live with no edits.
  In the finale she is freed, and a second, tiny glow beside her reveals a baby.
- **3 badges, the Wren twist** ("The Family Special"): Maxx's tearful "Family Special" stream begs his runaway little sister "Wrennie" to come home, with a baby photo. It's Wren. Their real surname is Bottomley. They once nursed a possum together; then the channel ate him. She found the first cage room on the *Vantadome* (it is on **Deck 5**; the Sorry Script's scribbled-out "Version 27: Say sorry for what's on Deck 5 of the Vantadome. ONLY IF THEY FIND IT." seeds it), took his map and ran. You decide she stays. Hook: Kaydence Kray knocks on the hull and offers to help.
- **Between 3 and 7 badges**, Kaydence travels with the crew, "helping", which mostly means filming everything for her channel, her *Clickbait* tied alongside.
- **5 badges, BOLT-9 switches sides** ("Very Good, Sir. No.", *written*): Maxx sends BOLT-9 to fetch Wren home and orders him to delete his memories of her childhood (he was her nanny-bot). BOLT-9 projects one memory onto the sail first: little Wren and little Max feeding a baby possum, **Sir Nibbles**, in a shoebox, and Max promising to "save every animal in the world". He refuses ("Very good, sir. No."), dusts Maxx off the screen and joins the crew. He has recorded everything Maxx has ever done. Kaydence asks him a little too eagerly where Maxx keeps things; he rates only her ring light. Hook: BOLT-9 tells you the photo of the *Soggy Biscuit* that Maxx showed in the prologue was sent to Maxx by someone, on a call BOLT-9 was sent out of the room for, while Kaydence's camera blinks behind you.
- **7 badges, Kaydence double-crosses** ("Stay Cray!", *written*): the crew's evidence box is an old biscuit tin labelled **"NOT BISCUITS"**, kept in the fridge behind Mo's leftovers because nobody goes near Mo's leftovers. Kaydence films where it lives, steals it overnight and leaves on the *Clickbait* ("Thanks for the snacks! Stay cray! xx K."). On Maxx's stream from the *Vantadome* he shreds it, and she gets the co-host spot on his finale. She gloats that she sent him the photo of the *Soggy Biscuit* back in the prologue. BOLT-9's memory is the backup: he dusted every item in the tin and scans everything he dusts ("She did not take the butler. I rate her plan four out of ten."). Hook: a gold net the size of a footy field is craned onto the *Vantadome*, something huge glows beneath it, and BOLT-9 still has a key to the yacht. These chapters never list which pieces of evidence were in the tin, because the Islands done by then differ.
- **Finale** (`finale`, order 99, unlocks when every marked Island's badge is won; *written*), "Biggest Rescue EVER, Live!": night, the *Vantadome* lit like a gold crown, forty million watching, Kaydence co-hosting, Old Glimmer in the footy-field gold net while deck speakers play her own song back to her. The *Soggy Biscuit* creeps in lights-off, with a little fleet of every beaten Keeper behind it (barnacled ship, tin bathtub, red umbrella on a raft). Tully: "four per cent chance" (the prologue's odds); Gus: "Four is more than none." Indi's last disguise: gold tracksuits made of chocolate wrappers, Mo's labelled "GOLDIE (MO)". BOLT-9's old key opens a hatch by the waterslide; Wren leads them to **Deck 5** ("This is where I found them. This is why I ran."), and this time her hands don't shake; Clawsette snips the locks. **Wren seeds** (one per Island episode at most): she knows things she shouldn't (where the vents are, his password habits, that he ticks lists in gold pen, that he sings flat, that he hides important papers in a box marked boring, that he's scared stiff of frogs, that he hates the cold and sends drones instead, that he always draws himself taller, that he never deletes a single second of himself), flinches at his catchphrases. They must read fine both before and after the reveal, so the crew never stares or asks how she knows: she says it quietly, or so that only you hear. "Lucky guess" is kept to three Chapters so it still means something: between-arrive and structure-arrive, where she says it to a Keeper, and the Captain's look back in twist-wren. Don't add more.

## Every Chapter

The same events at all four Bands, only the words change. Band 1 is about 250 words of short, plain sentences; Band 4 is about 700 with richer words and longer sentences. Separate paragraphs with a blank line. Band 1 and 2 keep every beat a later Chapter depends on: the prologue gives each crew member one line (Tully counts, Gus loves gross science, Zed games, Indi draws, Mo cooks badly, Wren looks after hurt animals), twist-bolt names Sir Nibbles, and the finale says SIRNIBBLES is the possum's name, shows the memory and keeps "You promised". Find the funny: something gross, something Maxx says, something Mo says. End on a hook: a reveal, a threat or a question that makes the reader tap the next Chapter.

**Band 4 is a stretch, not just a longer Band 3.** Same events, but:
- Mix short lines with longer sentences that hold an idea across clauses (a fronted "when" or "because" clause, an appositive, an "-ing" phrase).
- Leave some inference to the reader. Show feelings through action rather than naming them, and let the reader join up a clue nobody explains, such as Wren's seed or Tully's tally (IN: three. OUT: none.).
- Give each Chapter one layer only a strong reader will catch. Examples: the "HAPPY HOME" signs that all face the visitors, not the cages; the flat-pack boxes exactly the size of a cage.
