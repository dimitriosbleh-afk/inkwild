## 1. 4.1-b4-5 (Band 4)



**Match each simile to what it tells you about the robot.**

- The Goldbot X shot across the deck like a cheetah chasing its dinner. → **how fast it is**
- The cleaning drone zipped between the cabins like a dragonfly over a pond. → **how fast it is**
- The guard robot was as large as an elephant standing on its hind legs. → **how big it is**
- Its alarm wailed like a fire engine's siren in a narrow tunnel. → **how loud it is**
- The beeping went off like a dozen alarm clocks at once. → **how loud it is**
- Its gold armour gleamed like a freshly polished mirror. → **how shiny it is**
- The spy drone darted round the mast like a swallow chasing midges. → **how fast it is**
- The cargo robot filled the corridor like a fridge stuck in a lift. → **how big it is**
- Its visor glittered like sunlight on a swimming pool. → **how shiny it is**

Why: Each simile picks something famous for one quality: a cheetah's speed, an elephant's size, a siren's noise, a mirror's shine.

## 3. 4.5-b4-4 (Band 4)

> Crew, I have finally beaten Level 50 of Swamp Dash, and I need to tell you about it. First of all, the final boss, a giant robot crocodile, had about nine billion health points, and every jump needed perfect timing, down to a millionth of a second. I'm fairly sure I started this level back in Year 2. While I was waiting for the last checkpoint to load, my apple went mouldy and then turned into soil. Gus brought me a sandwich when I started, and by the time I finished, he had grown a long grey beard. When I finally landed the last hit, I cheered so loudly that the street lights in the whole town flickered, and the controller buzzed loud enough to wake every sleeping baby in Victoria. For the record, the level has twelve checkpoints, three hidden keys and a lava bridge that collapses if you stand still. I found two keys on my own and the third with a guide. Tully says I owe her a new pair of earmuffs. Now I'm going to lie down for about a decade.

**Match each hyperbole to what it really shows.**

- The boss had about nine billion health points. → **how hard it was**
- Every jump needed timing down to a millionth of a second. → **how hard it was**
- I started this level back in Year 2. → **how long it took**
- My apple went mouldy and turned into soil while it loaded. → **how long it took**
- By the time I finished, Gus had grown a long grey beard. → **how long it took**
- I cheered so loudly that the street lights in the whole town flickered. → **how loud it was**
- The controller buzzed loud enough to wake every sleeping baby in Victoria. → **how loud it was**

Why: Each stretch makes one thing bigger: how hard the level was, how long it took, or how loud it got.

## 4. 4.9-b4-3 (Band 4)



**Match each line to how the critic feels about the art.**

- The sculptor's slender figures stretch gracefully towards the light. → **admires it**

- The painter's bold colours leap off the canvas. → **admires it**
- His garish colours shriek at you from across the room. → **looks down on it**

## 5. 4.10-b3-2 (Band 3)



**Tap the cliché, then choose a fresh picture made from something at the picnic.**

Text: "For Indi's birthday, Mo made a pavlova and carried it all the way down to the beach. It was so windy that the picnic rug kept trying to fly away. The sand stung their legs like a swarm of tiny bees. Mo set the pavlova down on a plate and stepped back to admire it. It was tall and white, with strawberries on top. The cream was piled as high as a wave about to break. Then a gust of wind lifted the whole thing an inch off the plate. It was as light as a feather. Indi grabbed it with both hands just in time. Her fingers sank in like boots into fresh snow. Everyone ate it with sand on top. Mo said the sand was extra crunch. Nobody believed him, but nobody stopped eating either. Indi said it was the best pavlova she had ever eaten, and also the sandiest. Tully counted forty-three grains of sand on her slice."

Wrong spot: **It was as light as a feather.**, fix: **as light as the picnic rug flapping in the wind** (other fixes: as light as air, as light as a paper plane on a breezy day, as light as a balloon at a birthday party)

Why: The wind kept lifting the picnic rug, and now it lifts the pavlova, so the rug picture fits.

## 6. 4.5-b3-2 (Band 3)
